AI digest: Claude Ship code agents, Sierra at $30B, Jensen Huang back at Nvidia

Nov 24, 2026

Anthropic lets AI agents fix and approve code, Sierra raises $1.75B for customer-service agents, and Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO.

Anthropic's Claude Ship lets agents review, fix and approve code

Anthropic on Monday introduced Claude Ship, AI agents that take routine code changes from review to approval. Next to the existing Code Review, the new PR Steward (beta) repairs failing CI checks, resolves merge conflicts and responds to review feedback. Claude Approvals (beta) signs off on changes according to a policy that the team writes in plain language and keeps in the repository. It is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories; Anthropic advises starting in observe-only mode.

Sierra doubles its valuation to $30 billion

Sierra, the customer-service agent company co-founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, raised $1.75 billion in a Series F led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed, TechCrunch reports. That doubles its valuation to $30 billion in about six months. According to Sierra, outcome-based pricing helped double its revenue run rate to $400 million, and it works with almost half of the Fortune 50. The round suggests investors see customer support as one of the first areas where AI agents pay off.

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23 to bring back Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member, according to a company press release filed with the SEC. The board says it received input from senior levels of the US government and concluded that the decision poses no national security risk; the investigation led by independent directors will continue. The board thanked Colette Kress, who led the company as interim CEO while remaining CFO. For the AI industry, the move restores continuity at the top of its most important chip supplier.

What it means for business

AI agents are turning from helpers into participants in real workflows: they fix code, resolve support requests and approve changes within limits set by people. The practical lesson for companies is to define the rules first — what an agent may do alone, what goes to a human, and how each action is logged and reversed. Start with one narrow, measurable process, run the agent in observe-only mode and widen its permissions only after the results hold up.
